# Mountain Hike Challenge — Scene Design & Storyline

## Narrative Overview

A 7th grade student stands at the base of Mount Timpanogos. As they hike up a steep trail, their body begins working harder than usual. An AI guide appears and invites them to shrink inside their own body to see what is happening at the organ level. Students travel through five scenes, interacting with the organ systems that are responding to the demands of the climb. After completing the interactions, they return to the mountain and reach the summit, watching all systems work together in real time.

### Learning Progression

| Stage | What Happens |
|---|---|
| Observe | Students notice changes during the hike |
| Question | Why is the body changing? |
| Explore | Travel inside the body |
| Interact | Complete embodied VR interactions |
| Apply | Return to the mountain and complete the climb |
| Reflect | Explain how body systems work together during exercise |

---

## Perspective Design

The experience uses two distinct perspective modes:

**First-person hiker (Scenes 1–2 opening):** The student IS the hiker. They walk the trail, feel the incline, experience the physical demands of the climb. This builds emotional context and the motivating question — why does my body feel this way?

**Shrunk inside the body (Scenes 1–5 interactions):** The student shrinks to organ scale. The organ they are interacting with fills most of their field of view and feels massive. They can physically look around the environment to discover how systems connect. Artery tubes, nerve pathways, and tissue structures are visible in the surrounding environment — when the student pumps the heart, they can look up and see blood packets traveling toward the lungs, or look down and see them arriving at leg muscle tissue in the distance.

This spatial layout is the primary mechanism for showing how systems work together. The student discovers connections by turning their head and looking, not by being told.

## Scene Breakdown

### Scene 1 — The Trailhead
**Systems:** Nervous + Skeletal  
**Location:** Base of Mount Timpanogos, exterior mountain trail  
**Perspective:** First-person hiker transitioning to inside the body

**Story:** The student stands at the base of the mountain. The trail looks steep. The brain decides it is time to move. The AI guide appears and explains that before the hiker can take a single step, the nervous system has to send a signal and the skeletal system has to lock joints into place.

**Interactions:**
- Reach out and tap glowing neurons in the brain to send an electrical signal down the spinal cord
- Use hands to lock the knee joints into place to stabilize the body for the first big step

**Visual Environment:**
- Brain visible as a large glowing structure overhead
- Spinal cord visible as a lit pathway running downward
- Knee joint visible below as interlocking bones that click into place when activated

**AI Guide Actions:**
- Points to its own head to indicate where the brain is
- Traces a gesture down its spine to show signal direction
- Invites student to mirror the gesture on their own body

**Learning Outcome:** Students learn that a single step requires the nervous system to send a signal AND the skeletal system to provide structure simultaneously.

---

### Scene 2 — The Steep Incline
**Systems:** Muscular + Circulatory  
**Location:** Inside the chest cavity and leg muscle tissue  
**Perspective:** Shrunk inside the body, heart in foreground, pathways visible in environment

**Story:** The trail gets harder. The legs are burning. Muscles need more fuel — oxygen and glucose — to keep going. The heart is falling behind. The AI guide appears next to the heart and explains that the student needs to pump faster to keep up with the demand.

**Interactions:**
- Grab muscle fibers and pull them together to simulate contraction, helping the hiker lift their leg
- Use both hands to squeeze the heart in a steady rhythm — the faster the pump, the faster blood packets carry oxygen to the tired leg muscles

**Visual Environment:**
- Heart is the central focus object, large and interactive, positioned in front of the student
- Looking up and left: lungs visible as large glowing structures with artery tubes running toward them from the heart
- Looking down: leg muscle tissue visible in the distance with artery tubes running toward it
- Blood packets (discrete glowing dots) travel visibly along the artery tubes when the heart is pumped
- When pumping rate increases, blood packets move faster along both pathways simultaneously
- Leg muscle tissue brightens/pulses as oxygen arrives

**HUD Dashboard (corner of view):**
- Small body outline showing heart (red), lungs (blue), legs (green)
- Heart rate meter — rises with pumping
- O₂ delivery meter — only fills when pumping rate is sufficient
- Both meters update in real time

**AI Guide Actions:**
- Gestures toward the heart to focus attention
- Points up toward the lungs while saying "look up — watch what happens when you pump faster"
- Points down toward the leg muscles while saying "look down — see the oxygen arriving"
- Corrects the misconception: "Notice the heart isn't making new blood — it's pumping blood that already exists"

**Learning Outcome:** Students learn that the heart pumps oxygen-rich blood to muscles AND lungs simultaneously. Pumping faster increases delivery to both destinations at once.

**Interdependence mechanism:** The O₂ meter to muscles only fills when BOTH the heart is pumping AND the lungs are providing oxygen. If either stops, the meter drops. This makes the circulatory-respiratory dependency tangible rather than just stated.

---

### Scene 3 — The Thin Air
**Systems:** Respiratory  
**Location:** Inside the lungs  
**Perspective:** Shrunk inside the lung cavity, alveoli visible as large structures

**Story:** The hiker has reached high altitude where the air is thin. They are huffing and puffing. The AI guide explains that the diaphragm is the engine of breathing and that at this altitude, the student needs to work harder to pull in enough oxygen.

**Interactions:**
- Place hands on the diaphragm and push down hard to expand the lungs and draw in air
- Inside the alveoli, use a swiping motion to grab oxygen molecules from the air and push them into the bloodstream
- Toss carbon dioxide molecules outward to exhale

**Visual Environment:**
- Diaphragm visible as a large dome-shaped structure below the lungs
- Lungs expand visibly when the diaphragm is pushed down
- Alveoli visible as clusters of small sacs — when the student swipes, O₂ particles transfer into glowing blood vessels
- CO₂ particles float away when tossed out
- Blood vessels leading away from the alveoli connect back to the heart tubes visible in the environment

**AI Guide Actions:**
- Places its own hands on its chest/diaphragm area and pushes down, demonstrating the gesture
- Points to the alveoli clusters and explains "these tiny sacs are where the trade happens — oxygen in, carbon dioxide out"
- Gestures toward the blood vessels leading away: "that oxygen is now headed to your heart, and then to your legs"

**Learning Outcome:** Students learn that the diaphragm pulls air into the lungs, oxygen transfers into the bloodstream at the alveoli, and this directly supplies the blood that the heart then pumps to muscles.

---

### Scene 4 — The Energy Crash
**Systems:** Digestive  
**Location:** Inside the digestive system, small intestine  
**Perspective:** Inside the intestinal cavity

**Story:** Halfway up the mountain the hiker feels weak. They eat a granola bar. The food needs to be broken down and the glucose delivered to the leg muscles fast. The AI guide explains that digestion is not just about the stomach — most of the important work happens in the small intestine.

**Interactions:**
- Use a kneading motion to push food through the small intestine (peristalsis)
- Use fingers to pinch glucose molecules and pull them through the intestinal wall into nearby blood vessels
- Watch the energy meter fill as glucose enters the bloodstream and travels toward the legs

**Visual Environment:**
- Small intestine as a large tunnel-like environment
- Food particles visible moving through with peristalsis motion
- Intestinal wall visible as a textured surface with small openings
- Blood vessels visible just beyond the wall — glucose particles appear inside them when pulled through
- Energy meter fills progressively

**AI Guide Actions:**
- Corrects the misconception: "Most digestion doesn't happen in the stomach — the small intestine is where the real work gets done"
- Points to the intestinal wall: "see these tiny openings? Nutrients pass through here directly into the blood"
- Gestures toward the blood vessels: "that glucose is going to travel through the circulatory system all the way to your leg muscles"

**Learning Outcome:** Students learn that digestion provides glucose that enters the bloodstream and that this same circulatory system delivers it to muscles — directly connecting digestive and circulatory systems.

---

### Scene 5 — The Summit
**Systems:** Homeostasis (all systems)  
**Location:** Summit of Mount Timpanogos  
**Perspective:** Returns to first-person hiker perspective

**Story:** The student returns to the mountain and completes the final climb. All six body systems now respond together in real time on the live dashboard. The body is sweating to cool down, the heart rate is slowing, and everything is returning to balance — homeostasis.

**Interactions:**
- Walk uphill using natural walking gestures or controller movement
- Watch all body systems respond together on the live dashboard
- Sweep sweat from the skin surface to show how the body cools itself
- Conduct a system check by touching the brain, heart, and lungs to see them glowing in a calm, steady rhythm

**Visual Environment:**
- Mountain summit exterior, wide open sky
- Full live dashboard showing all active systems simultaneously
- Body systems pulse in coordinated rhythm as homeostasis is achieved

**AI Guide Final Line:**
"Excellent work. Every step up this mountain depended on your muscles, bones, lungs, heart, brain, and digestive system working together. No single body system could have completed this climb alone."

**Learning Outcome:** Students observe homeostasis as the body returning to balance after exertion, and see all systems coordinating rather than operating independently.

## Transition: Mountain to Inside the Body

When the steep incline scene begins and the hiker is clearly struggling, the AI guide appears and says:

"Have you ever wondered what is actually happening inside your body right now? Let's find out."

A glowing portal opens in the trail ahead. The student steps through and shrinks down to organ scale, entering the chest cavity. The mountain trail is visible in the far background through a translucent body outline, grounding the student in the context of the hike even while inside the body.
