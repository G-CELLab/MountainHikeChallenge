# Mountain Hike Challenge — AI Guide Script & Dialogue

## AI Guide Design Principles

The AI guide is a central character in the experience. Its role goes beyond narration — it is responsible for directing student attention, correcting misconceptions before they form, and making the connections between body systems explicit. The guide uses coordinated gestures alongside dialogue (gesture engineering) to reinforce understanding through physical demonstration.

### Core Behaviors

- **Gesture before speaking.** The guide performs a physical gesture to direct attention to the relevant organ or pathway before narrating what is happening there. Students look where the guide points before they hear the explanation.
- **Mirror invitation.** After demonstrating a gesture (e.g. placing hands on the chest to indicate lung location), the guide invites the student to mirror the same gesture on their own body.
- **Misconception interception.** The guide proactively addresses common misconceptions at the moment they are most likely to form — not in a separate correction, but woven into the natural explanation.
- **Cross-system bridging.** At every interaction, the guide explicitly narrates the connection to at least one other system. No interaction ends without the student hearing how this system depends on or supports another.
- **Encouragement on progress.** The guide gives real-time positive reinforcement tied to observable outcomes ("look — the oxygen is reaching the muscles now") rather than generic praise.

### Guide Gesture Vocabulary

| Gesture | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Point to own chest | "Your heart is here" |
| Hands on chest, push rhythmically | Demonstrate heart pumping |
| Hands on lower chest/abdomen, push down | Demonstrate diaphragm expansion |
| Trace finger down spine | Show nerve signal traveling downward |
| Gesture between two structures | Show connection between two systems |
| Sweep arm along artery tube | Show blood flow direction |
| Open palm toward student | Invitation to mirror gesture |

### Bootstrap / Loading Screen

### Scene 1 — The Trailhead

**[Guide appears beside the student at the base of the trail]**

> "Welcome to Mount Timpanogos. Today your mission is to reach the summit. But before you take your first step — something incredible is already happening inside you."

**[Guide points to its own head]**

> "Your brain just decided to move. Right now it's sending an electrical signal — think of it like a text message — all the way down your spinal cord to your legs."

**[Guide traces finger slowly down its spine — invites student to mirror]**

> "Place your hand on your own spine. Feel where that signal is traveling."

**[Neurons appear as glowing points in the environment]**

> "Reach out and tap those neurons to send the signal. Watch it travel."

**[Student taps neurons — signal travels visibly down the spinal cord]**

**[Guide gestures down toward the knee joint structure]**

> "Now look down. Before your leg can move, your skeletal system has to lock these joints into place. Without your bones providing that structure, your muscles would have nothing to pull against."

**[Student locks knee joints — clicking sound, joint stabilizes]**

> "There it is. One step requires your nervous system AND your skeletal system working at exactly the same time. Let's keep climbing."

### Scene 2 — The Steep Incline (Opening — on the mountain)

**[Hiker perspective, trail getting steeper]**

> "Feel that? Your legs are working harder. Your heart is beating faster. Your body knows the demand has increased — and every system is responding."

**[Guide appears]**

> "Have you ever wondered what is actually happening inside your body right now? Let's find out."

**[Portal opens — student steps through — shrinks inside the chest cavity]**

---

### Scene 2 — Inside the Body (Heart Interaction)

**[Guide is standing beside the heart, which fills the student's view. Artery tubes are visible running upward toward the lungs and downward toward leg muscle tissue in the environment.]**

**[Guide places both hands on its own chest in a rhythmic pumping motion]**

> "This is your heart. And right now it is not keeping up. Your leg muscles are demanding more oxygen than it is delivering."

**[Guide gestures toward the heart — invites student to approach]**

> "Place both hands here and squeeze in a steady rhythm. The faster you pump, the more oxygen reaches your muscles."

**[Student begins pumping — blood packets appear and travel along the artery tubes]**

> "Look up."

**[Guide points upward toward the lungs in the environment]**

> "See those? Your lungs. Watch the blood packets arriving there — picking up oxygen before coming back."

**[Student observes — then guide points downward]**

> "Now look down. See your leg muscles in the distance? Watch what happens when you pump faster."

**[Student pumps faster — blood packets move more quickly, leg muscles brighten]**

> "There it is. One pump, two destinations — lungs and legs — at exactly the same time."

**[MISCONCEPTION INTERCEPTION]**

> "One important thing — notice your heart is not making new blood. It is pumping blood that already exists. The heart is a pump, not a factory."

**[O₂ meter visible on HUD — partially filled]**

> "Keep pumping. The muscles need more. Watch that meter."

**[Student pumps to threshold — meter fills — success]**

> "Perfect rhythm. Your muscles have the oxygen they need. Now let's look at where that oxygen actually comes from."

---

### Scene 3 — The Thin Air (Inside the Lungs)

**[Guide appears inside the lung environment. Diaphragm is visible as a large dome below. Alveoli clusters are visible as glowing sacs.]**

**[Guide places hands on its own lower chest and pushes down slowly]**

> "We are inside your lungs now. At this altitude the air is thin — you need to work harder to pull enough oxygen in."

> "This dome below us is your diaphragm. When it moves downward, it creates space and air rushes into the lungs. Try it."

**[Student pushes diaphragm down — lungs expand visibly, air rushes in]**

> "Good. Now look at these structures around you."

**[Guide gestures to the alveoli clusters]**

> "These are alveoli — tiny air sacs. This is where the actual exchange happens. Oxygen from the air moves into your blood here, and carbon dioxide moves out."

**[Student swipes to move O₂ particles into blood vessels, tosses CO₂ out]**

> "See those blood vessels carrying the oxygen away? That oxygen is now heading back to the heart — and from there, directly to your leg muscles."

**[Guide gestures along the blood vessel pathways leading out of the lungs]**

> "Your respiratory system and your circulatory system are one continuous loop. The lungs cannot do their job without the heart, and the heart cannot do its job without the lungs."

**[MISCONCEPTION INTERCEPTION — if student appears to focus only on lungs]**

> "Breathing faster does not automatically help unless the heart is also pumping fast enough to carry that oxygen where it needs to go. Both systems have to work together."

---

### Scene 4 — The Energy Crash (Inside the Digestive System)

**[Guide appears inside the small intestine environment]**

**[Guide gestures at the intestinal walls]**

> "The hiker just ate a granola bar. That food needs to become fuel for the leg muscles — and fast. But here is something most people do not realize:"

**[MISCONCEPTION INTERCEPTION]**

> "Most digestion does not happen in the stomach. The stomach is just the beginning. The real work happens here — in the small intestine."

**[Student uses kneading motion — food moves through the intestine]**

> "That wave motion is called peristalsis. Your intestinal muscles squeeze in sequence to move food along."

**[Guide points to the intestinal wall]**

> "See those tiny openings in the wall? This is where nutrients pass directly into your bloodstream. Try it — pinch those glucose molecules and pull them through."

**[Student pulls glucose through the intestinal wall — it enters blood vessels]**

> "There. That glucose is now in your circulatory system. It is going to travel through your blood all the way to your leg muscles and give them the energy to finish the climb."

**[Energy meter fills progressively]**

> "Your digestive system just handed off to your circulatory system. Every system in your body is part of one connected network."

---

### Scene 5 — The Summit

**[Student returns to mountain perspective — begins final climb]**

> "You know what is happening now. Every step depends on your nervous system sending the signal, your skeletal system providing the structure, your muscles doing the work, your heart delivering the fuel, your lungs supplying the oxygen, and your digestive system providing the energy."

**[Dashboard shows all systems active simultaneously]**

**[Student reaches summit]**

> "Excellent work. Every step up this mountain depended on your muscles, bones, lungs, heart, brain, and digestive system working together. No single body system could have completed this climb alone."

**[System check interaction — student touches brain, heart, lungs — each glows calm and steady]**

> "Feel that? Everything is slowing down, evening out, returning to balance. This is homeostasis — your body finding its equilibrium again. It never stops working to keep you balanced."

## Dialogue Writing Guidelines

When writing additional guide lines or extending the script:

- Keep individual lines short — no more than two sentences before pausing for student interaction or observation
- Always follow a gesture with a verbal explanation, not the other way around
- Reference what the student can physically see in the environment ("look up," "see those structures," "watch that meter")
- Connect to at least one other system in every scene transition
- Address the misconceptions list from the project overview proactively — do not wait for the student to form the wrong idea
- Use present tense — things are happening now, not in abstract
- Avoid passive voice — "your heart pumps blood" not "blood is pumped by the heart"
