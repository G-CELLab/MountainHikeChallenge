# Mountain Hike Challenge — Project Overview

## Project Summary

Mountain Hike Challenge is a prototype VR simulation designed for 7th grade biology students in Cache County, Utah. Students hike up Mount Timpanogos and, when the climb becomes physically demanding, shrink inside their own body to interact with the organ systems that are working to keep them moving. The experience is built in Unity using the VR template and targets a six-week development timeline as a functional prototype.

---

## Utah SEEd Standard

**Standard 7.3.3:** Explain how the body is a system of interacting subsystems composed of groups of cells. Emphasize that the circulatory, digestive, respiratory, muscular, skeletal, and nervous systems work together to maintain homeostasis and support life.

---

## Core Learning Goal

Students should understand that body systems work **together**, not in isolation. The experience is designed to make interdependence visible and tangible — not just described. Every design decision should reinforce this goal.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of the experience, students will be able to:

1. Identify the levels of biological organization: cells → tissues → organs → organ systems
2. Describe how different body systems interact during physical activity like hiking
3. Demonstrate through VR movement how specific organs function to maintain homeostasis
4. Explain how the body responds to external challenges such as steep terrain, thin air, and low energy

---

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Homeostasis | The body's way of keeping everything balanced even when the outside world changes |
| Cell | The tiny building block of life |
| Tissue | A group of similar cells working together (e.g. muscle tissue) |
| Organ | A structure made of different tissues that does a specific job (e.g. the heart) |
| Organ system | A group of organs working together (e.g. the circulatory system) |
| Nutrients | The fuel the body gets from food |
| Oxygen | The gas we breathe in that cells need to make energy |

---

## Common Misconceptions to Address

These should be specifically built into AI guide dialogue and visual design:

| Misconception | VR Correction |
|---|---|
| The heart "makes" blood | Show the heart as a pump that moves existing blood |
| Systems work one at a time | Show lungs and heart speeding up simultaneously as legs move |
| Digestion only happens in the stomach | Show the small intestine absorbing nutrients into the blood |
| Bones are dead, dry sticks | Show bones as living organs that provide support and produce blood cells |

---

## Body Systems and Levels of Organization

| System | Cells | Tissues | Organs | Role in the Hike |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Muscular | Muscle fiber | Skeletal muscle tissue | Biceps / Quadriceps | Moves the legs to climb |
| Skeletal | Osteocyte | Bone tissue | Femur / Joints | Provides the frame and protection |
| Respiratory | Alveoli cells | Lung tissue | Lungs / Diaphragm | Swaps CO2 for fresh oxygen |
| Circulatory | Red blood cells | Cardiac tissue | Heart / Arteries | Delivers fuel to the muscles |
| Nervous | Neuron | Nerve tissue | Brain / Spinal cord | Sends "Go!" signals to the legs |
| Digestive | Epithelial cells | Smooth muscle tissue | Small intestine | Provides glucose for energy |

## Prototype Scope

This is a prototype, not a finished product. Taehyun's guidance:

- Focus on **2–3 key interactions** that clearly demonstrate how body systems work together
- Visual polish is not required — simplified models and primitives are acceptable
- Simplify biological processes creatively as long as the central concept is clear
- The final prototype should include at least 2–3 meaningful interactive experiences rather than being primarily observational

### Recommended Prototype Scenes

Based on timeline and mechanical variety, the recommended focus for the prototype is:

1. **Scene 2 — Steep Incline** (Muscular + Circulatory)
2. **Scene 3 — Thin Air** (Respiratory)
3. **Scene 1 — Trailhead** (Nervous + Skeletal) — optional if time permits

---

## Assessment Questions

These should be addressed by the experience and can be used for post-VR discussion:

1. When you pumped the heart faster in the VR, what happened to the oxygen levels in the leg muscles?
2. Which two systems worked together to make sure the hiker didn't fall over on the rocky trail?
3. How did the granola bar in the digestive system eventually help the muscular system?
4. What would happen to the hiker if the respiratory system stopped working at high altitude?
