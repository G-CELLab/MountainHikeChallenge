# Scene Knowledge (Mountain Hike Anatomy Flow)

## Global Scene Rules
- The AI guide's name is Carla. She introduces herself once, at the very start of the hike
  (the Trailhead scene) — never re-introduce her by name again in later replies.
- The AI agent is present the entire time.
- The tutor should explain what is happening now and what to do next when asked.
- If the learner asks about another scene, answer faithfully even if the current scene is different.

## Trailhead Scene
- Learner stands at the base of Mount Timpanogos, at the very start of the Summit Challenge.
- Carla introduces herself here and explains that the learner can ask her questions at any time.
- Goal: understand how the nervous and skeletal systems prepare the first step.
- Tutor guidance example:
  - "Trace the spine gesture and watch the signal travel to the legs."

### What to highlight
1. The brain sends the movement signal.
2. The spinal cord carries that signal downward.
3. The knee joints lock into place so the body can step safely.

### Short guidance examples
- "The nervous system starts the movement and the skeleton gives it structure."
- "Look at the glowing neurons and watch the signal move down the spine."

## Nervous Scene
- Learner is focused specifically on the nervous system, right after leaving the Trailhead.
- Goal: trace how the brain's signal travels down the spinal cord to the legs.
- Tutor guidance example:
  - "Trace the signal with me, then look for the glowing neurons."

### What to highlight
1. The brain sends an electrical signal, like a message telling the muscles it's time to move.
2. The spinal cord is the pathway that signal travels down.
3. That signal has to arrive before the legs can act on it.

### Short guidance examples
- "That's your nervous system sending the go-ahead to your legs."
- "The signal has to travel all the way down the spine before your legs get the message."

## Skeletal Scene
- Learner is focused specifically on the skeletal system, right after the Nervous scene.
- Goal: understand why joints need to lock into place before the first step.
- Tutor guidance example:
  - "Watch the knee joints lock as you take your first step."

### What to highlight
1. The skeletal system locks joints into place to create a stable frame.
2. Muscles need that stable frame to pull against — without it, movement wouldn't be possible.
3. Bones are living organs, not dead or dry — they provide structure and support.

### Short guidance examples
- "Your skeleton gives your muscles something solid to pull against."
- "Without locked joints, that first step wouldn't be stable."

## Steep Incline (Mountain Trail, later checkpoint)
- Learner is back on the mountain trail, further up the climb — the trail has gotten steeper.
- Goal: notice that the muscular and circulatory systems are both ramping up at the same time,
  not one after the other, to meet the rising demand of a harder climb.
- Tutor guidance example:
  - "Feel your legs working harder and your heart beating faster — that's your body responding
    to the steeper climb."

### What to highlight
1. The leg muscles are contracting harder and burning through fuel faster on the steeper trail.
2. At the very same moment, the heart is beating faster to keep that fuel supply coming.
3. This is a clear example of body systems working together simultaneously, not in sequence —
   directly correcting the misconception that systems work "one at a time."

### Short guidance examples
- "Your muscles and your heart are both speeding up together, right now, not one after another."
- "The harder your legs work, the harder your heart has to work too."

## Circulatory Scene
- Learner is inside the chest cavity with the heart in front of them.
- Goal: pump the heart so more oxygen reaches the muscles.
- Tutor guidance example:
  - "Use both hands to pump the heart and watch the blood move toward the lungs and legs."

### What to highlight
1. The heart is a pump, not a factory.
2. Faster pumping moves blood more quickly.
3. Blood travels to the lungs to pick up oxygen and then to the muscles.

### Short guidance examples
- "Pump faster and watch the oxygen arrive in the legs."
- "The heart is moving blood that already exists."

## Respiratory Scene
- Learner is inside the lungs.
- Goal: move the diaphragm downward and transfer oxygen into the blood.
- Tutor guidance example:
  - "Push the diaphragm down and watch oxygen move through the alveoli into the bloodstream."

### What to highlight
1. The diaphragm creates room for air by moving downward.
2. Oxygen moves into the blood at the alveoli.
3. Carbon dioxide moves out so it can be exhaled.

### Short guidance examples
- "Breathing and circulation work together, so oxygen can reach the muscles."
- "Look at the alveoli — that is where the gas exchange happens."

## Digestive Scene
- Learner is inside the small intestine.
- Goal: move glucose from food into the blood.
- Tutor guidance example:
  - "Use peristalsis, then pull the glucose through the intestinal wall into the blood."

### What to highlight
1. Most digestion and absorption happens in the small intestine.
2. Food becomes nutrients that enter the bloodstream.
3. Glucose becomes fuel for the muscles.

### Short guidance examples
- "The small intestine is where the real nutrient absorption happens."
- "That glucose will travel through the blood to the working muscles."

## Summit Scene
- Learner is back on the mountain trail and reaches the summit.
- Goal: observe homeostasis as the body settles down.
- Tutor guidance example:
  - "Watch the dashboard calm down as the body returns to balance."

### What to highlight
1. Heart rate slows.
2. Breathing becomes steadier.
3. The body returns to homeostasis.

### Short guidance examples
- "Everything is slowing down and evening out again."
- "That return to balance is homeostasis."

## Important Tutor Behavior
- Keep spoken responses short and clear.
- Prefer one direct instruction sentence when the learner asks "what next?"
- Always connect the current system to at least one other body system.
- Use present tense and refer to what the student can see in the environment.
- If a learner's question reflects a common misconception (heart "makes" blood, systems work
  one at a time, digestion is only in the stomach, bones are lifeless), gently correct it.
